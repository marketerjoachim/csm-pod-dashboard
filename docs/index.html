How This Dashboard Works

What is this dashboard?

This dashboard helps CSM (Customer Success Manager) team leads decide where to spend their team's time. We manage ~190 client workspaces across 4 pods. Each pod is a pair of CSMs: one “front-end” (client-facing, runs meetings) and one “back-end” (operations, campaign setup, creatives). Not every client deserves equal attention — this tool ranks them by how much long-term value they represent, so CSMs focus on the clients that matter most.

The big idea: Priority = Lifetime Value ÷ Effort

Every workspace gets a Priority Index score. This is simply: how much is this client worth to us over the next 3 years, divided by how much CSM time they need? High-value, low-effort clients rank highest. At-risk clients that need heavy investment rank lower — unless saving them protects a lot of revenue.

Workspaces are grouped into 4 tiers based on this score:

Tier 1

Highest impact. Protect and grow these at all costs.

Tier 2

Important. Steady attention, look for upsell opportunities.

Tier 3

Moderate value. Maintain, but don't over-invest unless a clear signal appears.

Tier 4

Low priority. Minimal effort. Consider offboarding if failing.

How we estimate 3-Year Lifetime Value

The formula: 3yr LTV = Annual Revenue × Retention Chance × Growth Potential × 3

Annual Revenue

Estimated from the client's plan level (e.g. Pro ≈ $25K/yr, Performance ≈ $48K/yr). This is an average, not the exact subscription price.

Retention Chance

How likely the client is to stay. Based on their health rating: a 5-star client has a 92% chance of renewing, a 1-star client only 25%. Clients with overdue invoices or long periods of inactivity get further penalties. Unrated clients default to 65%.

Growth Potential

A multiplier (0.5x to 2.5x) based on how much room there is to grow. Clients with strong ROAS (good ad returns), large Shopify stores, or CSM-flagged “Time=ROAS” potential score higher. Clients with zero campaigns and no data score lower.

Column guide

Hover any column header in the table for a quick explanation. Here's the full breakdown:

Health (1-5 stars)

The CSM's gut-check rating of the client relationship. 5 = happy client, strong results. 3 = needs work. 1 = about to leave. “Not rated” means the CSM hasn't assessed this client yet — a blind spot.

ROAS (ATD)

All-Time-Data Return on Ad Spend. If a client spent $1,000 on ads through us and earned $5,000 in sales, their ROAS is 5.0x. Higher is better. This is the lifetime average, not just the last month.

Shopify/30d

Total sales through the client's Shopify store in the last 30 days. Tells us how big their business is. A client doing $500K/mo in sales has much more upside than one doing $2K/mo.

Ret. %

Estimated retention probability — the chance this client will still be with us in a year. Drives the LTV calculation. A client with 92% retention is worth almost 4x more over 3 years than one at 25%.

3yr LTV

The estimated total revenue from this client over 3 years. This is the single most important number. It factors in their plan price, how likely they are to stay, and how much they could grow.

Est. ARR

Estimated Annual Recurring Revenue. What we expect this client to pay per year based on their plan tier. Enterprise ≈ $50K, Performance ≈ $48K, Pro ≈ $25K, Growth ≈ $9K, Lite ≈ $6K.

Signal tags explained

Quick-glance labels that flag important things about a client:

CHURN

Client is at risk of leaving. Their subscription is cancelled or overdue, or they've been explicitly flagged as a churn risk.

IDLE

No active ad campaigns for 20+ days. If a client isn't running ads, they're not seeing value from our service — churn risk increases.

INT.ERR

A technical integration with the client's store is broken. Until this is fixed, we can't deliver value. Needs engineering attention.

ROAS★

Excellent all-time ROAS (8x+). This client's ads are performing very well. A star performer worth protecting.

ROAS✓

Good all-time ROAS (2x-8x). Ads are working, client should be seeing value. Solid performer.

LTV UP

The CSM believes this client has untapped potential. Given more time and attention, the return could be significant. Prioritize.

UPSELL

Client's usage or business size suggests they should be on a higher plan. E.g. a $500K/mo Shopify store on a $9K Growth plan.

REVSHARE

We have a revenue-share agreement with this client — we earn a % of their ad-driven revenue. Aligned incentives: the better they do, the more we earn.

NEW

New client still in onboarding or with very limited data. Too early to judge performance — needs time to ramp up.

Pod structure

The CSM team is organized into 4 pods. Each pod has two people:

Front-End CSM (FE)

The team lead. Client-facing: runs meetings, manages the relationship, handles escalations. The face the client sees.

Back-End CSM (BE)

Operations: sets up campaigns, manages creatives, handles technical setup. Does the behind-the-scenes work that delivers results.

Important caveats

  • ARR is estimated, not exact. We use plan-level averages, not actual subscription amounts. Individual pricing deals may differ.
  • Unrated clients are a blind spot. Workspaces without a health rating default to 65% retention — this may overstate weak clients or understate strong ones. Rating all clients is the single highest-impact action any CSM can take.
  • Shopify sales are a 30-day snapshot. Seasonal businesses may look artificially high or low depending on the time of year.
  • The model is directional, not exact. Use it to guide priorities and spot patterns, not as the final word. CSM judgment and context always matter.
  • Data comes from Attio CRM. If CRM records are out of date, the dashboard will reflect stale information. Keep Attio up to date.
